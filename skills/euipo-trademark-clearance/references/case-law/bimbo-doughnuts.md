# Bimbo v OHIM ("BIMBO DOUGHNUTS") — C-591/12 P (8 May 2014)

**Court:** Court of Justice (appeal)
**Topic:** Refining Medion — "independent distinctive role" is not automatic

## Facts

Bimbo applied for "BIMBO DOUGHNUTS" for pastry goods; opposition based on the earlier Spanish mark "DOGHNUTS". The appeal concerned whether the Medion "independent distinctive role" doctrine applies mechanically whenever an earlier mark appears within a later composite sign.

## Holding / principle

1. **No automatic rule.** A finding of an **independent distinctive role** for one element of a composite sign is **not an exception** to the requirement to assess the **overall impression**; it cannot be applied automatically (para. 24–26).
2. The existence of an independent distinctive role must be **established case by case** on the facts; it does not follow merely because the later sign contains the earlier mark.
3. Likelihood of confusion must always be determined **globally**, considering all factors and the overall impression — Medion did not displace that.

## Application in a clearance analysis

- The **counterweight to Medion**: when a proposed mark contains an earlier mark, do not conclude "conflict" automatically — and do not conclude "cleared" automatically either.
- In **Step 2/Step 5**, state explicitly *whether* the earlier element keeps an independent distinctive role in the proposed sign, and *why*, as part of the overall-impression assessment — rather than treating it as a rule that decides the case.

## Key paragraphs
- Para. 24–26 — independent distinctive role assessed case by case within the overall impression; not automatic
