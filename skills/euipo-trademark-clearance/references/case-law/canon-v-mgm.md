# Canon v MGM — C-39/97 (29 September 1998)

**Court:** Court of Justice (preliminary ruling)
**Topic:** Factors for similarity of goods/services · interdependence · distinctiveness of the earlier mark · "same or economically-linked undertakings"

## Facts

MGM applied to register "CANNON" for films/video. Canon KK opposed on its earlier "Canon" marks. The dispute turned on how to assess similarity of goods and the role of the earlier mark's distinctiveness.

## Holding / principle

1. **Factors for similarity of goods/services.** All relevant factors relating to the goods/services themselves must be taken into account, including their **nature, intended purpose, method of use** and whether they are in **competition** with each other or are **complementary** (para. 23). (Later practice adds distribution channels and usual origin.)
2. **Interdependence.** The global assessment implies **interdependence** between the relevant factors: a lesser degree of similarity between the goods/services may be **offset** by a greater degree of similarity between the marks, and vice versa (para. 17).
3. **Distinctiveness widens protection.** Marks with a **highly distinctive character**, either per se or because of their reputation, enjoy **broader protection** than marks with a less distinctive character (para. 18).
4. **Test for confusion.** There is a likelihood of confusion where the public could believe the goods/services come from the **same undertaking or from economically-linked undertakings** (para. 29–30).
5. A likelihood of confusion can exist **even where the goods are not identical**, provided the degree of similarity, combined with the distinctiveness of the earlier mark, is sufficient.

## Application in a clearance analysis

- This is the **engine of Step 3** (similarity of goods/services): run the Canon factors rather than relying on Nice class numbers, which are not conclusive.
- Use **interdependence** explicitly when scoring overall risk: identical goods can raise the risk even with only moderate sign similarity, and a strong, well-known earlier mark stretches its protection across more distant goods.
- The "economically-linked undertakings" formulation is the correct articulation of the confusion test — broader than "identical source".
