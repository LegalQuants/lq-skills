# Medion v Thomson ("THOMSON LIFE") — C-120/04 (6 October 2005)

**Court:** Court of Justice (preliminary ruling)
**Topic:** An earlier mark retaining an *independent distinctive role* inside a composite later sign

## Facts

Medion owned the mark "LIFE" for electronics. Thomson used "THOMSON LIFE" for similar goods. The later sign combined a well-known company name (THOMSON) with the earlier mark (LIFE). The question: can there be a likelihood of confusion where the earlier mark is **not the dominant element** of the composite sign, but merely one component?

## Holding / principle

1. **Independent distinctive role.** Where goods/services are identical, a likelihood of confusion may exist where the later composite sign is made up of the third party's company name **plus** the earlier registered mark, and the earlier mark, **though not dominant**, still retains an **independent distinctive role** within the composite sign (para. 30–37, operative ruling).
2. This is an exception to the rule that the comparison rests on the overall impression: confusion is not excluded merely because the earlier mark does not dominate the later sign.

## Application in a clearance analysis

- Critical for **Step 2 (sign similarity)** when the proposed mark **swallows an earlier mark** into a longer sign (e.g. "[HouseBrand] AURORA"). Adding a house mark or company name does **not** automatically clear the conflict.
- Ask: does the earlier mark keep an independent distinctive role inside the proposed sign? If yes — especially with identical goods — the risk stays live.
- Read with **Bimbo**: the independent distinctive role is **not automatic**; it depends on the overall impression case by case.

## Key paragraphs
- Para. 30–37 — independent distinctive role; confusion possible though the earlier mark is not dominant
- Operative ruling — the THOMSON LIFE principle
