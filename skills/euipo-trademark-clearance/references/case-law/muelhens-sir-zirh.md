# Mülhens v OHIM ("SIR" / "ZIRH") — C-206/04 P (23 March 2006)

**Court:** Court of Justice (appeal)
**Topic:** Aural similarity counteracted by conceptual and visual differences · overall assessment

## Facts

Opposition to "ZIRH" based on the earlier mark "SIR" for perfumery/cosmetics-type goods. The marks were aurally similar in some languages, but visually distinct and conceptually different ("SIR" having a clear meaning).

## Holding / principle

1. **Aural similarity is not decisive on its own.** Aural similarity is only **one** of the relevant factors in the global assessment; it does not automatically lead to a likelihood of confusion (para. 21–22).
2. **Counteraction by visual/conceptual differences.** The **visual and conceptual differences** between two signs may **counteract** their aural similarities, **provided** that at least one of the signs has, for the relevant public, a **clear and specific meaning** grasped immediately (para. 35).
3. The marketing context (e.g. goods typically chosen visually) can reinforce the weight of visual differences over aural similarity.

## Application in a clearance analysis

- The mirror image of Lloyd: aural similarity **can** create confusion (Lloyd), but it **can also be neutralised** by clear visual and conceptual differences (Mülhens). Use both, depending on the facts.
- In **Step 5**, when a conflict rests mainly on phonetics, ask whether visual and conceptual differences — plus how the goods are actually chosen (by eye vs by ear) — counteract that aural overlap.

## Key paragraphs
- Para. 21–22 — aural similarity is one factor, not automatically decisive
- Para. 35 — visual/conceptual differences may counteract aural similarity where a sign has a clear, immediate meaning
