# OHIM v Shaker ("LIMONCELLO") — C-334/05 P (12 June 2007)

**Court:** Court of Justice (appeal)
**Topic:** Overall impression vs assessment on the dominant element alone

## Facts

A dispute over a figurative mark for a lemon liqueur ("Limoncello"). The General Court had assessed similarity by focusing on what it treated as the dominant component. On appeal, the question was when it is permissible to base the comparison on the dominant element rather than the whole sign.

## Holding / principle

1. **Overall impression is the rule.** The global assessment of likelihood of confusion must, as regards visual, aural and conceptual similarity, be based on the **overall impression** given by the marks, bearing in mind their distinctive and dominant components (para. 35, restating SABEL).
2. **Dominant-element shortcut is narrow.** Assessing similarity on the **dominant component alone** is permissible **only if all the other components are negligible** (para. 41–42). It is only when every other component is negligible that the comparison can be carried out solely on the dominant element.

## Application in a clearance analysis

- Guards against a common error in **Step 2**: do **not** reduce a sign to its "main word" and ignore the rest unless the other elements really are negligible.
- When a proposed mark shares a dominant word with an earlier mark but adds non-negligible matter, the added matter must be weighed in the overall impression — it can reduce (though not always eliminate) the risk.
- Pairs with **Medion** (a non-dominant earlier element can still count) to keep the analysis honest in both directions.

## Key paragraphs
- Para. 35 — overall impression; distinctive and dominant components
- Para. 41–42 — dominant-element assessment only where all other components are negligible
