# Ruiz-Picasso v OHIM ("PICASSO" / "PICARO") — C-361/04 P (12 January 2006)

**Court:** Court of Justice (appeal)
**Topic:** Conceptual differences counteracting visual/aural similarity · heightened attention for expensive goods

## Facts

The Picasso estate opposed registration of "PICARO" for vehicles, relying on the earlier mark "PICASSO". The marks were visually and aurally close, but "PICASSO" carries a clear, immediate meaning (the famous painter).

## Holding / principle

1. **Conceptual counteraction.** Where **at least one** of the marks has, from the relevant public's perspective, a **clear and specific meaning** that can be grasped **immediately**, the resulting **conceptual differences** may **counteract** the visual and aural similarities between the signs (para. 20).
2. **Attention level matters.** For goods such as **motor vehicles**, the average consumer's level of attention is **particularly high** at the point of purchase, given the price and technological nature of the goods (para. 38–39) — which reduces the likelihood of confusion.

## Application in a clearance analysis

- A powerful tool in **Step 2/Step 5** to **down-weight** a conflict: if the proposed mark or the earlier mark has an obvious, immediately grasped meaning that the other lacks, the conceptual gap can offset look-and-sound similarity.
- Combine with the **attention-level** point: high-involvement, expensive or specialist goods raise consumer attention and further reduce risk.
- Caution: counteraction requires the meaning to be **clear and immediate** to the relevant public — do not over-apply it to obscure or constructed meanings.

## Key paragraphs
- Para. 20 — conceptual differences may counteract visual/aural similarity where one mark has a clear, immediately grasped meaning
- Para. 38–39 — heightened attention for vehicles
