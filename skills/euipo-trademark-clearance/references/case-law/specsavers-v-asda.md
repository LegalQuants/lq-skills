# Specsavers v Asda — C-252/12 (18 July 2013)

**Court:** Court of Justice (preliminary ruling)
**Topic:** Colour and actual use affecting the global assessment of confusion and unfair advantage

## Facts

Specsavers held marks registered (in part) in black and white but used extensively in a particular green. Asda used a green logo in a marketing campaign. Questions arose about the relevance of the colour in which a mark is **actually used** to the likelihood-of-confusion and unfair-advantage analysis.

## Holding / principle

1. **Colour of actual use is relevant.** Where a mark is registered in black and white but has been **used extensively in a particular colour** such that the public associates that colour with the mark, the **colour the third party uses** is relevant to the global assessment of likelihood of confusion and of unfair advantage (operative ruling).
2. Likewise, the colour a third party itself uses, and any association the public makes between that colour and the third party, are relevant factors.
3. The assessment turns on the **perception of the relevant public** shaped by **actual use**, not only on the register as filed.

## Application in a clearance analysis

- A reminder that the register is a **starting point**, not the whole picture: how an earlier mark is **actually used** (colour, get-up, market recognition) can change the real-world risk.
- Reinforces the clearance limitation that **use evidence sits outside the register** — colour/use factors are **open factors** for the attorney to investigate before relying on a "looks different on paper" conclusion.
- Most directly relevant where marks are figurative/colour-dependent rather than plain word marks.

## Key paragraphs
- Operative ruling — colour of actual use of the earlier mark, and the colour used by the third party, are relevant to the global assessment
